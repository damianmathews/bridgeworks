The setup

Real ad spend, a chatbot, a Google sheet, a scheduling tool that doesn't talk to any of them.

The client is a regional services chain with four locations and about twenty trainers, running paid acquisition on Google and Meta and routing every inbound interaction through a chatbot on the WordPress site. Leads land in a Google sheet. The booking system is separate. The CRM is the sheet. The chatbot's primary job, in practice, is collecting names.

The diagnostic

We tested the bot with eight realistic prompts.

Three categories of question every real prospect or candidate would ask. Pricing. Services available. How to actually book a visit. The bot deflected, returned wrong information, or routed every path to the same name-capture form.

  • Critical questions failed hardest. Trainer rates, trial offers, and group fitness all dead-ended. These are the three questions the highest-intent prospects ask.
  • One answer was actively wrong. Asked about training prices, the bot quoted $40-60 figures. Those are the company's DIY plan prices, not training rates.
  • Every path dead-ends at a name form. When the bot can't answer, it asks for a name and email. There is no connection to booking, the scheduling tool, or any CRM. Trainer candidates and trial members all land on the same form.
The proposed system

One connected funnel. Every lead lands where a human can act on it.

The proposal replaces the chatbot and reorganizes the existing tools into a single funnel. The pieces talk to each other. The scheduling tool stays in place for existing-member sessions. The Google sheet is retired.

What stays human

The system handles the repetitive. People still own the relationships.

  • Trainer interviews and hires. The bot routes applicants. The owners still meet and decide.
  • In-person tours and trial sessions. Scheduling is automated. The actual visit stays with the team.
  • Pricing and policy decisions. The system reflects what the owners decide. It does not make pricing calls.
  • Brand voice and final approval on public content. Drafts are AI-assisted. Owners sign off before anything goes live.
The leaders in this category run the same stack at enterprise budgets. Equinox uses Salesforce Agentforce as a pre-sales agent. Gold's Gym SoCal switched to HubSpot and reported its best six months of sales after. The playbook works. Engagement model

Phased build. Light ongoing care.

  1. Foundation. Bot specced and built with vendor. CRM set up. Booking tool routed. Existing scheduling integrated.
  2. Connection. Follow-up sequences live. Reporting built. Front-desk team trained on the new flow.
  3. Operating. Steady-cadence content and ad ops. New workflows added as new repetitive tasks surface.
  4. Review. Performance evaluation. Decide what to expand. Decide what to drop. Repeat.
